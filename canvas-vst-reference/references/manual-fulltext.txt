CHAPTER 1

Welcome to Canvas

What is Canvas

Canvas transforms your mix into an interactive virtual soundstage. Every track becomes a colored,
breathing blob in an x-y-z canvas, drawn from its audio in real time. The canvas shows where it sits
in the stereo field (x), which frequencies it fills (y), and how loud it is (z).
Mix by moving the sounds themselves. Drag a blob to change its tone, stereo position and
perceived depth. Behind the scenes, every gesture becomes real EQ, pan, width, volume and
dynamics on the track.

Multi instance Consolidation

Put Canvas on each track you want to see. Every instance in the project sees the others, so
opening any single Canvas window shows the whole mix. You can edit any track from any window.

Formats & requirements

Canvas ships as an AU, VST3 and AAX plugin.

Installation & Setup

Installing

Download the installer from artdsp.net/downloads, run it, then restart your DAW (or rescan
plugins).

First run

The first time Canvas opens, your 14-day free trial starts. The trial is fully functional. A badge in
the top bar counts down the days. Open the license screen from that badge (or Settings >
License) and enter your serial key if you purchased. Activation is covered below.

Activating

Buy at artdsp.net and your serial key lands in your email right after checkout. Enter it in the
license screen (from the top-bar badge, or Settings > License). Activate in one window and every
other open instance picks it up immediately.

Machine slots

A serial covers 3 machines. Moving to a new computer? Deactivate on an old one to free the slot,
then activate on the new one.

Updates

Canvas checks for updates. When there's a new version, a green Update available badge
appears in the top bar, and once per DAW session you'll get a modal with the combined changelog
of everything you've missed. Downloads are at artdsp.net/downloads.
CHAPTER 3

Quick Start

Adding Canvas to your tracks

Insert Canvas on each track you want visualized, just like any insert effect. Last in the chain is
usually best so Canvas visualizes the track the way you hear it. Opening a single Canvas instance
allows you to see all of your instances. Each instance reads its track name from your DAW. If your
host doesn't send names, you can name tracks yourself; more in the side panel's right-click menu.

   Tip: If your tracks aren't showing up in the panel, make sure every track uses the same
   plugin format: VST3 instances only see other VST3 instances, AU only sees AU, AAX only
   sees AAX. Mixing formats splits your mix into separate rooms.

Reading the Canvas

The Canvas has three axes, and all three show what the audio is really doing.
    Left to right is stereo position. A blob near the left wall is a sound panned left.
    Up and down is frequency. The labels on the right wall run from 0 Hz at the floor to 18 kHz at
    the ceiling.
    Back and front is loudness. Louder sounds come toward you, quiet ones sink toward the back
    wall.

The shapes of the blobs mimic the audio you are hearing. A mono sound stands as a single
column. A hard-panned double shows two lobes with a gap between them. A blob's height covers
the frequencies the sound actually fills.

CHAPTER 5

Settings

The settings menu, opened from the gear in the top bar, holds Canvas's global preferences: how
the blobs render, what the toolbar shows, appearance, and your license.

                                                                          The gear menu

Source Blob

Controls various graphical configurations for the blobs.
    Form: How solid the body reads, from soft smoke to dense.
    Frequency Sensitivity: How much quiet spectral content shows. Low means only the loud core,
    high means every whisper.
    Width Averaging: At 0% the stereo image follows every hit live. At 100% you see the averaged
    stereo image.
    Smoothness: Motion damping, from twitchy to heavy glide.
    Hold Time How long the blob is visually held after its audio stops (Fader mode). How long the
    depth holds at peaks (Live mode).
    Depth: Controls how blob depth is derived. Fader: a blob's depth mirrors its volume knob. Live:
    Depth follows the measured loudness of your audio.
    Reset to defaults puts the Source Blob controls back to default.

Tools

The dock on the left edge of the Canvas is where the mixing happens. It
includes moving a sound's frequency via EQ, shaping its shelves, painting EQ
in or out, widening, panning and volume. Pick a tool, then work directly on the
selected sound. Every tool has a single-key shortcut, and a chip near the
cursor reads out live values while you work.
A few things hold true for the whole dock:

    Tools act on the selected sound. With nothing selected, the edit tools dim as
    a reminder to click a blob first.
    Scroll almost always does something useful: EQ gain on Freq, brush size on
    the brushes, shelf Q on Shelf, volume on Vol.
    Right-click a tool button to reset what that tool controls.

CHAPTER 6 · TOOLS

Select Q

                         Pads selected in the image. The pad blobs hold the glow, everything else steps back.

Click a blob to select it. The selected sound holds its glow while everything else steps back, its
panel row lights up, and the float panel appears with its knobs. Click empty space, the blob again,
or blank panel space to deselect.
Hold Shift and drag a blob to move it freely, frequency and pan together, without leaving the
Select tool. While you drag, the Freq and Pan buttons light up in the dock to show what the move is
driving.
The Select button shares its dock slot with two siblings. Hold the button to switch between them:

    Grab ( G ): drag a blob anywhere, frequency and pan together, no modifier needed.
    Auto Select ( X ): a toggle. While it's lit, clicking a blob selects it no matter which tool you're
    holding (the paint brushes are the exception, they keep painting).
CHAPTER 6 · TOOLS

Freq F

  Dragging the Pads. The chip reads out the frequency as you move, and the tinted band shows where the emphasis sits.

The Freq tool moves the sound's frequency emphasis, which uses a single static bell EQ curve,
placed wherever you drag.

    Click and drag up or down to move the frequency emphasis.
    Scroll up/down in the Canvas to adjust the intensity (the gain of the bell EQ).
    Scroll left/right in the Canvas to adjust the range of the emphasis (the Q of the bell EQ).
    The knobs in the bottom bar mirror what you're moving. Drag one for finer control, hold Shift
    while dragging for finer still, and double-click its value to type an exact number. This applies to
    all knobs and sliders in all tools.
    EXTREME dynamically EQ's the sound from a gentle push to full isolation. Everything outside
    the bell gets cut hard, up to 64 dB, so the sound only exists where you placed it. Inside the bell,
    Canvas measures how much of the sound's energy actually lives there and boosts by exactly
    what's needed to keep the sound at full loudness. A sparse band gets a big lift, a loaded band
    barely moves. This is great for forcing a sound into a specific pocket of the mix.
CHAPTER 6 · TOOLS

Shelf S

              The Highs and Lows pivot lines, with the shelf knobs (Gain, Freq, Q for each shelf) in the panel below.

The shelf allows you to quickly add/remove highs and/or lows to a specific sound. Two dashed
lines cross the canvas at the high-shelf and low-shelf cutoffs, and tinted zones show what each
shelf reaches. This tool allows you

    Drag up/down in the upper zone to brighten or soften the highs. The lower zone shapes the
    lows the same way.
    Drag a dashed line up/down to move that shelf's cutoff. A Hz readout rides along with the
    cursor.
    Scroll up/down inside a zone to set that shelf's Q, which is how steep the transition is. You'll
    see the zone's gradient tighten or relax.
    Shift-drag to stretch: both shelves move in opposition, so one gesture gets you brighter on top
    and softer below.
CHAPTER 6 · TOOLS

Add A

     Holding the Add brush on the Pads. The stream shows the band being painted, with a live Hz readout at the brush.

Add is a paint brush for EQ. Hold the mouse down over the frequencies you want more of and the
boost builds the longer you hold. Scroll to change the brush size.
Your strokes turn into editable EQ points (up to 12) which you can fine tune later in the EQ Editor.
Use the Clear Paint button to remove any boosts applied to the sound.

Subtract is the opposite of add. This tool allows you to hold your cursor on the canvas to carve
that band out of the sound.

Right-click on a selected blob

                                                Right-clicking the selected Pads blob on the Canvas.

Right-click the Canvas on a selected blob (or any blob under the cursor) and a small menu opens:
    Solo: mute every other Canvas track. Solo again to restore.
    Mute: mute this track. Its blob disappears instantly.
    Hide: hide the blob from the Canvas without touching the audio. Same as the eye in the track
    panel.
    Reset Blob: put every effect on this sound (EQ, shelves, pan, width, volume) back to defaults. It
    asks before it wipes anything.

The EQ Editor

       The EQ editor open on the selected Pads: five painted points sitting on the live curve, with the analyzer behind.

All EQ based adjustments, including everything you paint with Add and Subtract condenses into
editable EQ points on an EQ curve over the analyzer. Open the editor by clicking the small EQ
profile box above Auto-Gain (visible while a sound is selected); drag its corner to resize, double-
click to snap it back.

    Drag a point to move its frequency and gain.
    Scroll on a point to change its width.
    Double-click to delete it. Right-click for more options.
    Each point has numeric fields for frequency, gain and width when you want exact values.
    Points are host-automatable, so you can sweep them from your DAW.
While a sound is selected, three knobs also float over the Canvas: Focus (frequency), Q and
Intensity. Hover or drag to see a knob's value; double-click the knob to reset it, or double-click the
value to type an exact number. The EXTREME button next to them is covered on the Freq page.

Auto-Gain

Below the dock, under the EQ profile box, sits Auto-Gain. It keeps a track's loudness steady while
you work. Canvas matches the processed volume to what came in before the effects..

Disable

The power button at the very bottom of the dock bypasses effects on your track. Audio passes
through untouched and the blob turns grey while it's off.

The Track Panel

                                     Click the arrow handle on the right edge of the Canvas (or press
                                      Tab ) to open the track panel. It lists every Canvas instance in

                                     the project, one row per track, so you can manage the whole mix
                                     from any window. Drag the handle to resize the panel.

The arrow handle on the right edge.

What's in a row

                               A row: color stripe, name, SC badge, volume knob, mute, and visibility toggle.

    Name: your DAW's track name, synced to every window. If the host didn't provide one, the row
    shows "Instance N" and a plain click on the name lets you type one. Named rows are renamed
    from the right-click menu.
    SC: opens this track's "takes space from" editor. See Make Space.
    Volume knob: the track's post gain, per blob. It's the same parameter the Vol tool drags, just in
    knob form.
    Mute dot and eye: audio and visual mute/solo. Next section.

Mute & solo, audio and visual

Each row ends with two controls that mirror each other: the mute dot works on the audio, the eye
works on the view.

    Click the mute dot: mute or unmute the track. A muted track's blob disappears instantly.
    Right-click the mute dot: solo the audio. Everyone else mutes; right-click again to restore.
    Click the eye: hide or show the blob. Audio keeps playing; only the Canvas changes.
    Right-click the eye: solo the view. Every other blob hides.
    Hold Shift and the two become one button: a capsule wraps around the pair. Shift-click mutes
   and hides together; shift-right-click solos audio and view together.

Make Space (Sidechain Ducking)

The idea

Sometimes two sounds fight for the same space, like a kick and bass. Make Space allows spectral
ducking, where one track takes space from others exactly where its energy needs it, and only
while it's playing. You set it up from the track that takes the space, choosing which sounds duck for
it.

The Make Space pane                      Open it from a track's SC badge in the panel. Each linked
                                         track gets a card with:
  Kick taking space from the Pads: one
           live link with its controls.      On: enable or mute the link, and × to remove it.
                                             Amount: how much space gets taken.
                                             Attack / Release: how fast the space opens and closes.
                                             Trigger: Amplitude (smooth, follows the level) or Transient
                                             (snappy, follows the hits).

                                         The duck concentrates where the trigger's energy actually is,
                                         so a kick can take low-end room from a pad without touching
                                         its shimmer.
CHAPTER 8 · MAKE SPACE

Setting up links

Click + Add a track in the pane and pick the tracks that should give way. The SC badge on a
ducked track's row stays lit the whole time its space is being taken.

Reading the duck

                  The victim's EQ editor while ducking happens: the orange region is the space being taken, live.

    Open the ducked track's EQ editor and the duck draws in orange: a live curve dipping exactly
    where, and as hard as, the space is being taken.

Presets & Templates

Instance presets

Per-sound presets capture the full state of one track including EQ, panning, width. Use the "Apply
preset to ..." picker in the top bar and the save button next to it. Factory presets ship with the
plugin and keep themselves current. Your own can be saved, renamed and deleted. The Default
preset is the same thing as Reset Blob.

Mix templates

   Note: Mix templates are an experimental feature. They change parameters on every
   instance in your project at once, and level matching will adjust track volumes. You can undo
   the whole operation.

A mix template is a whole-mix starting point. Canvas analyzes your audio and applies a set of
filters, panning, width and sidechain routing to each track in your mix, keyed by what role the track
plays (kick, bass, vocal and so on). Apply one from the Mix template bar at the top of the track
panel.
CHAPTER 9 · PRESETS & TEMPLATES

Step by step

1. Open the Mix template menu. First time in a session, Canvas asks you to set your DAW faders
    to 0 dB (any value across the board will work) and make sure Canvas is last in each chain. Do
    both, then confirm.

Pick a template: factory ones under Art DSP, your own under Yours.

                                                 The setup check that opens the menu the first time.

3. Identify your tracks. Canvas tries its best to identify each track from its name. Unidentified
    tracks show in red under "No match, tag manually": make sure to categorize them with the type
    dropdown, or they get skipped. You can also change any automatically matched track types.

4. Click Apply and let your audio play. Canvas measures each track's loudness while it plays and
    tells you when it's ready.

5. Hit APPLY and watch your knobs change. Every matched track's EQ, pan, width, sidechain and
    volume glide to the template.
